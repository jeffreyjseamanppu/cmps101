1. Lecture: Introduction to AI and Machine Learning

Estimated time: 75 minutes

Learning objectives: By the end of this lesson, you should be able to define artificial intelligence and machine learning, distinguish common types of learning, describe generative AI, recognize applications in software development, and discuss ethical concerns.

What Is Artificial Intelligence?

Artificial intelligence (AI) is the field of developing computer systems that perform tasks associated with human intelligence, such as recognizing patterns, interpreting language, making predictions, and solving problems. Not every AI system learns from data; some use rules or search algorithms.

Examples: spam filters, voice assistants, fraud detection, recommendation systems, and chatbots.

AI vs. Machine Learning vs. Deep Learning

TermMeaningExample
Artificial IntelligenceBroad field of systems performing intelligent tasksGame-playing software
Machine Learning (ML)Methods that learn patterns from data to make predictions or decisionsEmail spam classifier
Deep LearningML using multilayer neural networksImage recognition
Generative AIModels that generate content such as text, images, or codeAI writing or coding assistant

Three Common Machine Learning Approaches

  • Supervised learning: Trains on labeled examples, such as emails marked spam or not spam.
  • Unsupervised learning: Finds patterns or groups in unlabeled data, such as customer segments.
  • Reinforcement learning: Learns through actions and feedback, such as an agent improving its performance in a simulation.

How Machine Learning Works

  1. Define a problem and identify useful data.
  2. Prepare and clean the data.
  3. Split data into training and evaluation sets.
  4. Train a model to recognize patterns.
  5. Evaluate accuracy, limitations, and possible bias.
  6. Deploy and monitor the system as conditions change.

Generative AI and Prompt Engineering

Generative AI can produce text, code, images, and other content from user instructions called prompts. A strong prompt provides context, a clear task, constraints, and the desired output format. Generated content can be inaccurate, so human review is essential.

Example prompt: “Explain what an IP address is to a first-year computer science student. Use one real-world analogy and a three-item summary.”

AI in Software Development and Cloud Computing

Developers use AI tools to brainstorm solutions, explain unfamiliar code, draft unit tests, and review documentation. Cloud platforms provide scalable computing, storage, and managed services used to train and host many AI applications. AI can also run on personal devices or local servers.

Ethics, Security, and Responsible Use

  • Accuracy: AI can generate plausible but false answers.
  • Bias: Training data can reflect unfair or incomplete patterns.
  • Privacy: Do not enter passwords, personal records, or confidential data into unapproved tools.
  • Copyright and attribution: Follow course policies and verify ownership and permitted use.
  • Accountability: People remain responsible for checking AI-assisted work.

2. Lab: Experimenting with Generative AI

Estimated time: 45–60 minutes | Format: Individual

Lab goal: Investigate how prompt specificity changes AI output, then evaluate the reliability of the responses. Use an instructor-approved AI tool. Do not create an account or enter personal data if course policy prohibits it; an instructor demonstration may be used instead.

Part A: Compare Two Prompts

  1. Open an approved generative AI chatbot.
  2. Enter the basic prompt below and save the response.
  3. Enter the improved prompt below and save the response.
  4. Compare clarity, accuracy, organization, and usefulness.

Basic prompt:

Explain cybersecurity.

Improved prompt:

Act as a college instructor. Explain cybersecurity to a first-year applied computer science student in 200 words or fewer. Include a definition, three common threats, and two everyday safety practices. Use simple language and bullet points.

Part B: Test a Technical Explanation

  1. Ask the AI to explain the difference between cloud computing and local computing.
  2. Request a comparison table with cost, scalability, security responsibility, and examples.
  3. Check at least two claims against course notes or a credible source.
  4. Record one helpful point and one omission, uncertainty, or error.

Part C: Improve a Prompt

Write your own prompt to help a beginner understand one topic: databases, networking, programming, or cybersecurity. Revise it after reviewing the first answer. Describe what changed and whether the revision improved the response.

Lab Submission

  • Both Part A prompts and a brief comparison (100–150 words).
  • The Part B AI response summary and two independently verified claims, with source links.
  • Your original and revised Part C prompts and a 100-word reflection.

Suggested grading: Prompt comparison (30 points), fact checking (30 points), revised prompt (25 points), reflection and completeness (15 points). Total: 100 points.

3. Discussion: Will AI Change the Future of Computer Science?

Purpose: Examine the opportunities and challenges created by AI for students, professionals, and society.

Discussion Prompt

AI tools can generate code, answer technical questions, summarize documents, and automate repetitive tasks. Some people believe these tools will improve productivity and create new jobs. Others worry about inaccurate output, reduced opportunities for entry-level workers, privacy, and overreliance on automation.

Respond to the following questions:

  1. Which area of computer science do you think AI will change the most, and why?
  2. Should software developers be allowed to use AI-generated code in professional projects? What safeguards should be required?
  3. What is one skill students should develop to remain valuable as AI tools improve?

Participation Requirements

  • Initial post: 200–250 words, with one concrete example.
  • Peer responses: Reply thoughtfully to at least two classmates (75–100 words each).
  • Support your position with reasoning and respectful discussion; cite sources if you use external facts.

Suggested grading: Initial post (60 points), two peer replies (30 points), clarity and professionalism (10 points). Total: 100 points.

4. Assignment: Research a Real-World AI Application

Objective: Explore how an AI application works, the problem it addresses, and its limitations.

Instructions

Select an AI-powered application or service. Examples include a chatbot, AI coding assistant, recommendation system, translation tool, image recognition service, or fraud detection system. Research the application using its official documentation and at least one additional credible source.

Your report must address all six areas:

  1. Overview: Name the application, its developer, and its primary purpose.
  2. Technology: Explain how AI is used. Identify whether it uses machine learning, generative AI, or another approach, if this information is publicly documented.
  3. Practical use: Describe a realistic scenario where a person or organization would use it.
  4. Benefits: Identify and explain at least two advantages.
  5. Limitations and risks: Discuss at least two concerns such as errors, bias, security, cost, or privacy.
  6. Career connection: Explain how this application might affect a future role in computer science or information technology.

Submission Requirements

  • Submit a 2–3 page report as PDF or Word, excluding references.
  • Use clear section headings corresponding to the six required areas.
  • Include at least two credible sources with links or citations.
  • If you use AI to assist your writing, disclose how you used it and independently verify factual claims.
  • Do not submit raw AI-generated text as your own analysis.
