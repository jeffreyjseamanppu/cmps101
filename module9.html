2. Lecture: Key Concepts

What is cloud computing?

Cloud computing provides on-demand access to computing resources—such as servers, storage, databases, and software—over a network. Instead of purchasing and maintaining every physical server, organizations can rent resources and scale them as needed.

Deployment models

  • Public cloud: Resources delivered by a third-party provider over shared infrastructure.
  • Private cloud: Cloud infrastructure dedicated to a single organization.
  • Hybrid cloud: A combination of private/on-premises and public cloud environments.

Service models

ModelWhat the provider deliversExample
IaaSVirtual servers, networking, and storage; customer manages operating systems and applicationsAzure Virtual Machines, Amazon EC2
PaaSManaged application runtime/platform; developers focus on their codeAzure App Service, Google App Engine
SaaSReady-to-use applications accessed through a browser or appMicrosoft 365, Google Workspace

Virtualization

A hypervisor allows multiple isolated virtual machines (VMs) to share the hardware of one physical computer. Each VM can have its own operating system, allocated memory, storage, and virtual networking. Virtualization helps providers use hardware efficiently, but a VM still requires configuration, maintenance, and security controls.

Cloud benefits and trade-offs

Benefits include scalability, rapid deployment, global reach, and reduced up-front hardware purchases. Trade-offs include recurring costs, internet dependence, vendor lock-in, data governance challenges, and the need for careful configuration.

Connection to last week's cybersecurity module

Cloud providers secure underlying infrastructure, while customers retain responsibilities that depend on the service model. Strong authentication, least-privilege access, encryption, backups, and monitoring remain important.

4. Guided Lab: Explore a Cloud Architecture (Ungraded)

Estimated time: 45–60 minutes. Materials: Browser, Microsoft Learn resources, and a document or drawing tool.

Important: No credit card, cloud subscription, or paid resources are required. Do not deploy a VM or activate a paid trial. The lab uses publicly accessible training materials and a paper/design simulation.
  1. Explore: Open the Microsoft Learn cloud computing module. Read the sections about deployment models and shared responsibility.
  2. Identify: Write down one real-world example each of IaaS, PaaS, and SaaS. Explain why each belongs in its category.
  3. Investigate VMs: Open Introduction to Azure Virtual Machines. Read about selecting a VM size, operating system, storage, and networking. Skip the optional deployment exercise.
  4. Watch: View the embedded Azure introduction video above, especially the Azure portal demonstration.
  5. Design: Imagine a small online bookstore. Draw a simple diagram with customers, a website/application, a database, file storage, and backup. Indicate which parts might run on a VM versus a managed cloud service.
  6. Secure: Add three security controls to the diagram (for example, multifactor authentication for administrators, role-based access, and backups).
  7. Reflect: In 100–150 words, explain one benefit and one risk of moving the bookstore to the cloud.

Lab completion check: Keep your diagram and reflection for use in Assignment 2. No separate lab submission is required.

5. Assignment 1: Cloud Services in Everyday Life — 50 Points

Purpose: Recognize cloud technology in familiar products and accurately classify services.

Instructions

  1. Select five cloud-based services you use or know about (for example, OneDrive, Google Docs, Netflix, Dropbox, or a cloud-hosted game).
  2. For each, identify the provider or company, its purpose, the data or resources involved, and the most appropriate cloud service model. Note that an end-user app may be SaaS even when its provider uses IaaS or PaaS behind the scenes.
  3. Choose two of your examples and explain how an outage or internet disruption might affect users.
  4. Explain two security or privacy precautions users should take when using cloud services.
  5. Write a 400–600 word report with a comparison table and at least two credible sources.

Submission: PDF or Word document uploaded to the LMS.

6. Assignment 2: Design a Small-Business Cloud Solution — 50 Points

Scenario: A small business with 10 employees needs a public website, shared files, a customer database, and reliable backups. The business has a limited IT budget and no full-time systems administrator.

Instructions

  1. Choose a business type (bookstore, café, retail shop, fitness studio, or another small business).
  2. Create a labeled architecture diagram showing users, the internet, an application/website, data storage, backups, and security controls.
  3. Choose a cloud provider (AWS, Azure, or Google Cloud) and identify at least three relevant services. Explain the purpose of each.
  4. Indicate which services represent IaaS, PaaS, or SaaS, where applicable.
  5. Explain four security practices, including authentication/access management and backup/recovery.
  6. Discuss two advantages, two disadvantages, and at least two factors that could affect monthly cost. No exact pricing is required.
  7. Submit the diagram plus a 400–600 word explanation with at least two sources.

Submission: One PDF containing the diagram and written explanation. You may use PowerPoint, Google Drawings, diagrams.net, or a neatly photographed hand-drawn diagram.
